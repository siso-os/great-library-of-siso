# Rights, Safety, and Ethics

## Default rule

Publish metadata, provenance, a careful summary, and an official locator. Copy or deeply extract a payload only after item-level rights and safety review.

“Public,” “declassified,” “FOIA-released,” “archival,” and “public domain” are different states.

## Rights review

For every object record:

1. identify the creator and jurisdiction;
2. identify the current custodian and any donor or deed restrictions;
3. separate U.S. government work from contractor, foreign, photographic, cartographic, news, literary, or other embedded material;
4. record the legal or licensed basis for reuse;
5. choose `metadata_only`, `link_only`, limited quotation, approved copy, prohibited copy, or pending;
6. retain takedown and correction paths.

Government custody does not prove that every embedded item is free of copyright or other rights.

## Privacy and living persons

Apply data minimization even when a record is officially online. Escalate:

- home addresses, personal contact data, identity numbers, financial data;
- medical, psychiatric, sexual, family, or intimate information;
- minors;
- victims, witnesses, informants, covert personnel, and vulnerable communities;
- allegations about living persons;
- biometric or precise-location data.

A name in an intelligence or law-enforcement file is not proof of guilt, affiliation, reliability, or agency employment. People Graph links require identity resolution and context.

## Reputational fairness

Clearly label allegations, source reports, analytic judgments, contested interpretations, and later corrections. Preserve exculpatory and contradictory material. Do not use a document title, folder label, watch-list entry, or redacted mention as a factual verdict about a person.

## Victims, trauma, and graphic material

Use the least graphic material needed for the research purpose. Prefer metadata and description over reproduction. Add content warnings, restrict access, or withhold imagery involving death, torture, abuse, medical procedures, children, or human remains. Historical interest alone is not sufficient.

## Operational security

Official release does not eliminate risk. Escalate current or plausibly current:

- source and method details;
- facility vulnerabilities or precise sensitive locations;
- identities that may not be safely publicized;
- targeting, evasion, surveillance, or collection procedures;
- authentication, cryptographic, communications, or security weaknesses;
- current force protection or emergency-response details.

Historical context may be summarized while operationally enabling detail is omitted.

## Hazardous technical content

Use metadata-only or high-level summary for material that could materially enable:

- nuclear weapons or safeguards defeat;
- biological or chemical weaponization;
- explosives construction;
- cyber exploitation or bypass of live systems;
- sabotage of critical infrastructure;
- acquisition or concealment of controlled materials.

Do not assemble a capability manual from individually public records.

## Authenticity and leaked material

This department is for official public records and clearly labeled secondary copies. It does not solicit, authenticate for publication, or launder material reasonably suspected to remain classified or unlawfully obtained.

A secondary copy is a lead until matched to an official identifier, custody trail, release letter, or authoritative record. Web-archive evidence can establish prior public availability but does not automatically authorize republication after withdrawal.

## Safety tiers

| Tier | Typical case | Public treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `standard` | ordinary historical record | metadata, link, reviewed summary |
| `enhanced_privacy` | living persons, victims, medical or family data | minimized metadata; redaction/escalation |
| `hazardous_technical` | operational or dual-use technical content | metadata and non-enabling summary |
| `restricted_metadata_only` | severe unresolved rights, privacy, safety, or access concern | minimum metadata or no public entry |

## Decision record

Every reviewed item records:

- rights status, basis, embedded-material review, and reuse decision;
- privacy, reputational, graphic/trauma, operational-security, and hazardous-technical results;
- reviewer and date;
- public treatment;
- unresolved questions;
- correction, removal, or appeal path.

## Ethical selection

The department must not become a sensational highlight reel. Preserve mundane provenance records, release manifests, null findings, contradictory evidence, and documents showing uncertainty or institutional error.

Curiosity is welcome. The public-interest test, source criticism, proportionality, and human dignity remain mandatory.
